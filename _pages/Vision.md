## Research Vision

My research aims to generate quantitative evidence to anticipate and respond to emerging and re-emerging infectious disease outbreaks. I focus on climate-sensitive arboviral diseases, using epidemiological data, statistical inference, and mathematical modelling to understand transmission dynamics, quantify disease burden, and evaluate prevention strategies.

My long-term goal is to connect three fundamental questions: **Where and when could outbreaks emerge? Who is most likely to be affected? Which interventions should be prioritised?** Building on research on chikungunya, I aim to develop approaches that can be extended to other arboviral threats in both endemic and previously non-endemic settings.

### Research Priorities

**Outbreak emergence and transmission dynamics**

Investigate how climate, vector suitability, pathogen introductions, and population immunity shape the risk, timing, and magnitude of infectious disease outbreaks.

**Disease burden and population vulnerability**

Estimate the short- and long-term health burden of infections, accounting for variation in exposure, age, underlying health conditions, and disease severity.

**Vaccination and epidemic preparedness**

Evaluate when, where, and whom to vaccinate, quantify vaccine benefits and risks, and identify evidence needed to inform surveillance and vaccine deployment decisions.
